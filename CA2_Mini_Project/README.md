## Results (tested on 2025, a year not used for mining or training)

| Task | Method | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Where | DBSCAN | 804 street-scale hotspots; 4.79× the 2025 KSI collisions per km² of random past sites; 93.89% of KSI collisions fall outside any hotspot |
| Why | Apriori | 107 of 109 rules kept their lift on 2025 |
| Segments | K-Modes, k = 6 | 60 rules appear only inside segments; 55 held on 2025 |
| Severity | Random Forest, best of 4 | ROC-AUC 0.679, PR-AUC 0.440 against 0.270 by chance |
| What next | Poisson boosting on 500 m cells | ROC-AUC 0.764 against 0.680 for a past-count list; 2.13 more points of KSI covered at 5,000 sites |

## Running

```bash
pip install -r ../requirements.txt
cd POC
jupyter notebook DMBI_CA2_Group09_BlackSpot_Miner.ipynb
```

The notebook downloads the data from the official source on first run and takes about 20 minutes. Seeds are fixed.

**Data:** Department for Transport, [Road Safety Open Data](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/road-safety-open-data) (STATS19), Open Government Licence v3.0.
**Reference paper:** Kumar, S. and Toshniwal, D. (2015). *Journal of Big Data*, 2, 26. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40537-015-0035-y

**Limits.** STATS19 covers police-reported injury collisions only and has no traffic volumes. Rules and models show association, not cause. The numbers describe Great Britain; the method could run on India's eDAR records, but the numbers do not transfer.
